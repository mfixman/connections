Axiom selector research plan — 2026-10-16

Motivation: Chvalovsky, Suda and Urban, Teaching Vampire New Tricks: An
Experimental Study of Neural Clause Selection (2026).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16228

Adapt the training and evaluation ideas to input-axiom scoring. Vampire's
derived-clause scorer is a different task; do not copy its architecture without
evidence that the extra machinery helps. Start with the existing network.
These are proposed experiments, not authorization to launch jobs or monitoring.

1. Diagnose buried core axioms first.
Score identical held-out problems with SmallFull, DefaultFull and SInE. Record
the worst-ranked axiom in an observed core, the smallest ranking prefix that
contains the whole core, and the number of core axioms receiving near-floor
guidance weights. Relate these to proof-search regressions and inspect examples
for biases against long clauses, equality axioms or particular encodings.
Recovery of one observed core does not measure all possible proofs.

2. Prove, collect, retrain: initially three rounds.
Train on the original unguided SAT-core examples. Run the guided prover on
training problems, including previously unsolved ones. Extract cores from new
successful searches and fine-tune on original plus new examples. Retain older
successful examples to reduce forgetting. Preserve alternative cores as separate
examples initially, rather than merging them into a union. Bound sampling per
problem so repeated successes do not overwhelm the dataset.
Control: give unguided search with fresh seeds the same extra collection budget,
then retrain equivalently. This separates learned exploration from more data.
Never collect training examples from validation or test problems. Record the
prover revision, policy, seed, matrix identity and collection round for each core.

3. Add a ranking-loss ablation after the collection-loop pilot.
Compare existing BCE with BCE plus lambda times a sampled pairwise ranking loss:
  mean softplus(score_noncore - score_core).
Sample pairs within each problem, emphasizing highly ranked non-core candidates,
and average the ranking term per problem. Tune lambda only on validation data.
Non-core means absent from this observed core, not useless in every proof.
Keep BCE initially: weighted search depends on score magnitudes as well as ranks.
Judge the change by end-to-end proof search, not ranking accuracy alone.

4. Vary the candidate axiom context.
Compare training on current problems, larger candidate sets, and an explicitly
balanced mixture. Test robustness by adding valid, previously available premises
from the same development, preserving symbol identities and logical context.
Do not concatenate unrelated TPTP files. Keep equivalent variants in one split.
Measure how existing axioms' ranks change as irrelevant candidates are added:
the current scorer mean-pools all candidate axioms, so the context changes too.
Distinguish already-selected (bushy) inputs from large-theory (chainy) inputs.
Treat this as a separate experiment from the ranking loss.

5. Measure complementarity under a fixed total budget.
Build a small validation-selected schedule from NoModel, SInE and the best neural
model. Compare against the strongest individual configuration at equal total
cost, including preprocessing and inference. Report unique gains, regressions
and cumulative solved-problem counts, not just the union of full-budget runs.
For Vampire integration, compare learned premise selection with both a fixed
strategy and a strong existing schedule.

Implementation order: diagnostics; three collection/retraining rounds; ranking
loss and candidate-context experiments separately; small schedule comparison.
Keep a genuinely untouched final test set. Select models on validation proof
performance and report seed variation, data coverage and input errors.
